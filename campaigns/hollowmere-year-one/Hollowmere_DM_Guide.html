Year One: The Portrait in the East Wing

New students arrive at Hollowmere, meet each other, and find out why things keep vanishing around the castle. The trail leads to a covered portrait in the closed East Wing, and to the lonely, cursed artist trapped inside it.

I. The Secret Story

What's really going on. The players uncover this piece by piece.

A hundred years ago, a quiet Tidewell student named Cordelia Vane could paint pictures that moved, breathed, and talked back. She had few friends. Her best friend was a Thornwood boy named Edwin Ashby, who spent hours sitting for portraits so she'd have someone to talk to.

In her fifth year, a bully tore up her sketchbook in front of the whole school. That night, Cordelia painted him. By morning he was stuck inside the canvas, banging on the paint. Teachers got him out, but Cordelia was expelled, and her paintings were locked in the East Wing under white sheets.

Before she left, Cordelia painted one last picture: a self-portrait, so that a part of her could stay at Hollowmere forever. Her grief soaked into the paint. She never signed it, and an unsigned living portrait never fully wakes. Edwin died years later and became the Belltower ghost. He still visits the covered portrait sometimes, but it has never answered.

This autumn, someone woke her up. A figure in a hood slipped into the East Wing and signed Cordelia's name in fresh paint, and beside it they painted a strange sigil: a closed eye inside a ring. The portrait woke up lonely and hungry for company. Now Cordelia's Shade reaches out of the paintings at night and pulls things in: things nobody is watching, then pets, and soon a person.

Who did it? The players won't learn that this year. The sigil belongs to the Hollow Circle, a secret group working toward something that grows across the whole campaign. It shows up once in Session 3 and again at the end of Session 5. Don't explain it. Let the players wonder.

Give each one a voice and a want. The "Knows" line is what they can tell the players.

Wants: To keep students safe without scaring them.

III. Places in Year One

The Sky-Ferry Marigold

An old flying riverboat pulled by four griffons. It has a deck, a passenger cabin, and a cramped luggage hold below.

Lantern Hall

The great dining hall. Four long house tables, a high table for teachers, and the Founders' Lantern hanging overhead. Portraits line the walls.

The Inkwell

The library, seven floors cut down into the island's rock. Floors 1–3 are open to first-years. Floor 4 and below is the Restricted Stacks, and the books there don't like to be read.

The Glasshouses

Greenhouses full of snapping, singing, and wandering plants. Pip's favorite drawing spot.

The Kitchens

Under Lantern Hall. Hot, busy, and run by hearth sprites. Dumbwaiter shafts run from here to every wing.

The Belltower

Home of the bell that rings for classes and curfew, and of Edwin the ghost, who sits at the top most nights.

The East Wing

Closed. Dusty corridors, sheet-covered paintings, and at the end of the Long Gallery, one tall portrait whose sheet has slipped off.

Inside the Portrait

A world made of paint: the Brushstroke Wood, the Wet-Paint Marsh, and the Painted Manor where Cordelia lives. Colors are too bright, shadows are painted on, and everything smells like linseed oil.

IV. Running School Life

Each session mixes three kinds of scenes: school (classes, meals, friends), mystery (clues and sneaking), and adventure (combat and danger). If a session feels slow, jump to the next adventure scene.

🌙 Curfew

After the Belltower rings ten, students sneaking around make a group Dexterity (Stealth) check, DC 12. If half or more succeed, they're unseen. If not, Hodge Barrow (or a prefect) spots them: they can run (Athletics or Acrobatics DC 13), talk their way out (Deception or Persuasion DC 14), or accept detention. Detention is never a dead end. It's a chance to find a clue.

🏮 The Lantern Cup

Lantern Cup: Each house starts with 100 points from the other students. Add the points each player earned to their own house. The house with the most points at the end of Session 5 wins. Every player in the winning house starts Year Two with Heroic Inspiration.

Session 1 The Sky-Ferry

Level 1. Players meet each other, learn combat basics, and find the first clue.

Dawn at the River Docks

Read aloudMist hangs over the river. Students in new robes clutch trunks and wands. Then, out of the clouds, comes an old riverboat with four griffons pulling it on silver chains, wings beating like thunder. A woman in goggles leans over the rail and shouts: "HOLLOWMERE STUDENTS! ALL ABOARD! KEEP AWAY FROM THE TAILS!"

Have each player describe their character as they climb aboard. Captain Wren Tallow points all the first-years to one cramped cabin.

The Cabin: Meeting Each Other

Go around the table. Each player answers one icebreaker question (roll a d6 or let them pick):

(1) What did you pack that you're secretly nervous about? (2) What magic happened to you before you got your letter? (3) Which house do you hope for, and which do you dread? (4) Who did you leave behind? (5) What's the first thing you want to learn? (6) What's your worst habit?

Then each player makes a bond with the player on their left: met on the docks · cousins · rivals from home · sat together on the ferry · owe each other a favor · secretly admire each other.

Meet Pip. A small, freckled girl sits in the corner, sketching each of them. If anyone talks to her, she shyly shows them their own portrait. It's very good. Meet Lucan. He swaggers in, mocks somebody's robes, and leaves to sit with "the important families."

🏆 House points: None yet. The houses don't exist for them until sorting.

Raid on the Luggage Hold

Read aloudA crash from below. Then giggling, high, buzzing, and mean. Captain Tallow swears. "PIXIES! In the hold again! Somebody get down there before they eat all the luggage!"

The luggage hold is 30 feet by 20 feet: dim, cramped, and stacked with trunks (half cover). The pixies are tearing through bags and flying off with small objects.

This is a teaching fight. Explain turns, attacks, and spells as you go. Pixies try to steal rather than hurt, and they flee when Bloodied. Players who throw sweets can win without fighting at all (see the pixies' Sweet Tooth trait).

⚔️ Encounter

Enemies: 6 × Luggage Pixie

Difficulty: Low: 200 XP with the reinforcements (exactly the Low budget for 4 level-1 characters).

Reinforcements: 2 more pixies burst out of a trunk at the start of round 3.

Tactics: Pixies swarm the student with the most stuff, Snatch an item, and fly toward the hatch. A pixie that reaches the hatch with an item escapes, and that item is lost until Session 4.

The Empty Trunk

After the fight, one trunk stands open and completely empty: Lucan's. Its inside is smeared with streaks of thick brown paint. The pixies didn't do this. Their tiny hands are clean.

Lucan storms in and accuses the players. A good Persuasion or Insight check (DC 12) calms him down, but he stays sour.

Clue (Investigation DC 12): The paint is still slightly wet and smells like old oil. The smear looks like a handprint, but with brushstrokes instead of fingerprints.

🏆 House points: Players who share their own things with Lucan or calm him down get a note: +10 house points once you're sorted.

First Sight of Hollowmere

Read aloudThe clouds part. Below you is a lake as black as ink. In the middle sits an island, and on the island a castle with crooked towers, a hundred lit windows, and one dark wing where no lights burn at all.

End the session here.

Session 2 Sorting Night and First Day

Level 1. Sort the students, show off school life, and make the vanishings personal.

The Founders' Lantern

Read aloudLantern Hall is louder than anywhere you've ever been, with four long tables, hundreds of students, and floating candles. Then the Headmistress taps her cane once, and the hall goes silent. Above you, the great Lantern begins to turn, and it burns in all four colors at once.

Call students up one at a time. Each steps into the light and says which house they want. The light flares in that color. (The player always chooses.)

Make it a moment: the Lantern whispers one word to each student that only they hear. Pick a word that hooks their backstory: Remember. Protect. Hidden. Brave. Listen. Home.

Lucan is sorted into Duskmoor and smirks. Pip goes to Thornwood and looks relieved. Then the house tables cheer and the feast appears.

🏆 House points: Award any house points promised in Session 1.

First Classes (Skill Challenges)

Run two or three classes. In each, every student makes three checks using the class's skill, and the DC is 13 unless noted. 3 successes = Outstanding (+10 points), 2 = Good, 0–1 = Try again. Let players suggest other skills with a good explanation.

Charms with Professor Marsh (Arcana): Make a feather float. Marsh explains that painted things can be undone with water or turpentine. Remember this.

Potions with Professor Fell (Medicine or Intelligence): Brew a Boil-Shrinking Tonic. On a failure, roll a d6: 1 hair turns green for a day · 2 hiccuping bubbles · 3 voice goes squeaky · 4 cauldron grows legs and walks off · 5 the potion smells like socks · 6 it explodes in glitter.

Creature Care with Professor Reed (Animal Handling): Feed a nervous baby griffon. Pip is great at this and smiles for the first time.

Things Start Vanishing

Over the next few days, the players notice (or hear about) the vanishings: Professor Marsh's spectacles are gone from her desk. The Lantern Hall salt cellar is missing. The Duskmoor banner is torn from the wall. Then Hodge Barrow's beloved toad, Mortimer, disappears from his lantern-lit office.

Every scene of a vanishing has a smear of the same brown paint.

Dueling Club (Optional)

Professor Cole runs a friendly dueling club after dinner. Lucan challenges one of the players. Use the Student Duelist stat block. Duels are to the first knockout: at 0 HP a duelist drops to 1 HP and yields.

A good duel, win or lose, earns +5 points. A graceful winner or a good loser earns +5 more.

⚔️ Encounter

Enemies: 1 × Student Duelist (Lucan Ashgrove)

Difficulty: One-on-one, friendly. Lucan plus two Duskmoor friends works as a 3-on-3 team duel.

Tactics: Lucan opens with Tripping Jinx, then fires Stinging Hex. He uses Flash Shield the first time he's hit.

The Painting That Watches

Read aloudOn your way back to your tower, you pass the portraits in the long corridor. One of them, a painting of an empty chair beside a window, has changed. There's a smear of brown paint on the chair now, and in the window's reflection, for just a second, a woman in old-fashioned robes is looking straight at you.

Then she's gone. End the session. Everyone reaches level 2.

⬆ Level 2 at the end of this session.

Session 3 Paint in the Halls

Level 2. Pip goes missing. The players investigate, learn who Cordelia was, and find a way into the East Wing.

Pip Is Missing

Read aloudBreakfast. The Thornwood table is whispering. Pip Larkspur's bed wasn't slept in. Her sketchbook was found in the corridor outside the East Wing, and the last page is smeared with brown paint.

Professor Reed is frantic. The teachers search the grounds and tell students to stay in their towers. Of course, the players won't.

Pip's sketchbook (if the players get hold of it): drawing after drawing of one portrait, a young woman with paint on her hands. On the last page, Pip wrote: "She says she's so lonely. I told her I'd come back."

The Investigation

Let the players go where they want. There are three questions to answer, and each has more than one source, so they can't get stuck. Each source can be visited in any order:

Question 1: What's taking things? · Professor Fell identifies the paint as Vane's Umber, made by a student a hundred years ago (Persuasion DC 12, or an Outstanding Potions grade). · Nettle the hearth sprite saw a lady step out of a picture frame carrying the salt cellar (offer a sweet, or Persuasion DC 13). · Pip's sketchbook shows the same portrait over and over.

Question 2: Who is she? · The Inkwell has an old record: "Cordelia Vane, Tidewell, expelled for the Living Portrait Incident" (Investigation DC 13). · The trophy room has an art prize engraved with her name. · Edwin the ghost tells the whole sad story if approached kindly. He's at the top of the Belltower after dark.

Question 3: How do we get in? · The East Wing door needs Hodge's key (borrow it by returning Mortimer later, sneak it while in detention with Sleight of Hand DC 14, or ask nicely after Pip vanishes). · Unlatcha (Knock) opens it. · The loose panel behind the Emberly trophy case (Professor Cole's old trick). · The kitchen dumbwaiter (Nettle shows students who were polite to her). · Inside, the portrait opens if a student puts a hand on the frame and says Cordelia's name (Edwin knows this, and so does Pip's sketchbook margin).

The Restricted Stacks

Read aloudFloor four of the Inkwell is cold and dark. As your light falls across the shelves, the books rustle. Pages tear loose by themselves, hundreds of them, swirling into the air like a flock of angry birds.

Players come here to find Cordelia's records, or a book called On Waking Paintings. The pages attack anyone who isn't a librarian.

Trick: A student can use an action to slam a big book shut on a swarm (Athletics DC 12), dealing 2d6 damage to it. Fire works well too, but if it hits the shelves, the librarian will not be happy (−10 points).

Villain seed: The library card in On Waking Paintings shows it was borrowed three weeks ago. The borrower didn't sign a name, just a small drawing of a closed eye inside a ring.

⚔️ Encounter

Enemies: 3 × Swarm of Restless Pages

Difficulty: Easy: 300 XP, just under the 2024 Low budget (400 XP for 4 level-2 characters). That's on purpose for a mystery night. Add a fourth swarm for 5 or more players.

Tactics: Two swarms attack at once, and the third tears loose at the start of round 2. They chase anyone holding a book.

Curfew

Most of this investigation happens at night. Use the curfew rules. Getting caught isn't the end: detention with Hodge Barrow means cleaning near the East Wing, which is a perfect chance to learn about the key.

The Long Gallery

Read aloudThe East Wing smells like dust and old paint. Painting after painting stands draped in white sheets. At the end of the corridor, one sheet has slipped to the floor. Behind it is a tall portrait of a young woman with paint-stained hands. Her name is signed in the corner in wet, fresh paint. And in the background of the painting, small and far away, you can see Pip, waving for help.

End the session on this image. When the players put a hand on the frame and say Cordelia, the paint ripples, and they fall in. Everyone reaches level 3 before Session 4 begins.

Session 4 Inside the Portrait

Level 3. Cross the painted world, find the lost things, and reach Cordelia's manor.

⬆ Level 3 at the start of this session. Students choose their Path (subclass). Unlock the Path box on their sheets.

Rules of the Painted World

Tell the players these rules as they discover them:

• Water and turpentine undo paint. A splash of water (or Create Water, Fizzwhit, or a full waterskin) erases a painted object up to the size of a door, or deals 2d6 Force damage to a painted creature.

• Paint is creative. Fizzwhit (Prestidigitation) can recolor or smudge small painted objects. A student who describes painting something useful (a bridge, a door, a rope) can try an Intelligence or Dexterity check (DC 13) to make it real for one minute.

• Nothing here is really alive except people who fell in. Painted creatures splash into puddles when defeated.

Umber the Painted Fox

Soon after arriving, the players meet Umber, a small brown fox painted by Cordelia long ago to keep her company. He's nervous and lonely too.

If the students are kind (Animal Handling or Persuasion DC 11), he guides them and warns them of danger. He knows: "Lady Cordelia isn't wicked. She just doesn't want to be alone. But the new paint, the fresh signature, made her hungry."

The Brushstroke Wood

Read aloudThe trees here are painted with thick, swirling strokes of green and gold. The sky is too blue. The ground squishes like wet canvas. And from between the trees, sleek wolves made of brown and black paint pad toward you, dripping as they come.

The wolves are guards. Umber can lead the group around them with a group Stealth check (DC 13), but if it fails, the wolves attack.

Lost items found here: the Duskmoor banner (tangled in a tree) and Lucan's trunk contents, scattered around a clearing.

⚔️ Encounter

Enemies: 4 × Brushwood Wolf

Difficulty: Easy warm-up: 400 XP, under the Low budget (600 XP for 4 level-3 characters). The marsh and the Frame Warden come next, so students should save their resources.

Terrain: Painted trees give half cover.

Tactics: Wolves circle and use Pack Tactics, gang up on one student, and flee once two of them are gone.

The Wet-Paint Marsh

Read aloudThe woods give way to a swamp of thick, shining paint. Puddles of every color bubble and pop. A river of clear liquid, which smells strongly of turpentine, cuts across the middle. On the far side stands a painted manor house.

Crossing the marsh: each student makes a Dexterity saving throw (DC 12) or gets stuck in wet paint (Restrained until they or an ally use an action to pull free, Athletics DC 12). Clever plans skip the save: painting a bridge, flying, using rope, or following Umber's path (Survival DC 12).

The turpentine river: it erases painted things. Wading through it is safe for real people but uncomfortable. Students can fill flasks or buckets here, which is a huge help in Session 5.

Lost item found here: Hodge's toad Mortimer, painted onto a lily pad and croaking sadly. A splash of water frees him.

The Painted Manor: The Gallery

Read aloudInside, the manor is one long hall of picture frames, hundreds of them, all empty. Your footsteps echo. Then one of the biggest frames, a huge gilded thing taller than a door, creaks, steps down off the wall, and comes toward you on its corners.

The Frame Warden guards the way to Cordelia's studio, along with two more painted wolves. Students who are pulled into its frame can be freed by beating it or breaking its grapple.

After the fight, the students find: Professor Marsh's spectacles, the salt cellar, three Mendwell Draughts (Potions of Healing), and Cordelia's diary (it gives Advantage on one talk-down check in Session 5).

End the session at the studio door. From behind it, they hear a woman humming, and Pip saying "Please. They're my friends. They'll come."

⚔️ Encounter

Enemies: 1 × Frame Warden, 2 × Brushwood Wolf

Difficulty: Low: 650 XP (Low is 600, Moderate is 900 for 4 level-3 characters).

Tactics: The Warden snares the closest spellcaster while the wolves keep everyone else busy.

Session 5 The Artist

Level 3. Face Cordelia's Shade, rescue Pip, and choose how this ends: by fighting or by kindness.

The Studio

Read aloudThe studio is enormous and full of light. Paint pots float in the air. In the middle of the room, on a tall easel, stands a self-portrait of a young woman, and in front of it stands the woman herself, made of paint and shadow, a brush in her hand. Pip is beside her, half-painted onto a canvas up to her shoulders. Cordelia smiles at you. "Oh, how wonderful. More friends. I'll paint you right next to her."

Give the players a moment to talk. Cordelia is polite but won't let anyone leave. If they're rude or attack, the fight begins. If they try talking right away, go straight to Talk Her Down. She'll listen for one round before getting impatient.

When the fight starts, she paints two Painted Duplicates, copies of two students, which step off a canvas to fight.

The Fight

The self-portrait on the easel keeps Cordelia's Shade alive (see her Living Portrait trait). Smart students will go for it. Umber the fox can hint: "Her picture! Her picture is her heart!"

Freeing Pip: a student next to Pip's canvas can take an action to scrub or splash her free (or succeed on an Athletics check, DC 13). A freed Pip hides and shouts encouragement, and she can hand someone her drawing.

Turpentine and water from the marsh deal 2d6 Force damage to the Duplicates and the Shade's portrait. Remind players if they filled flasks.

Found items: let each student roll on the Lost & Found table once at the start of the fight. They find something useful lying around the studio.

⚔️ Encounter

Enemies: 1 × Cordelia's Shade, 2 × Painted Duplicate

Difficulty: Between Moderate and High: 1,300 XP (Moderate is 900, High is 1,600 for 4 level-3 characters). Her self-portrait's healing makes it feel harder, and talking her down makes it easier.

Tactics: Cordelia stays at range, uses Brushstep to escape anyone who gets close, and saves Paint Them In for the student who hurts her most. The first time she's Bloodied, the room's paint runs (see her Tears of Paint trait) and she hesitates. This is the best moment to talk her down.

Talk Her Down

At any point, especially once she's Bloodied or after the students show her something from her past, players can try to reach Cordelia instead of fighting her.

How it works: 3 successes before 3 failures. On their turn, a student can use their action to talk (Insight, Persuasion, Performance, or History, DC 13). While a student is talking, Cordelia doesn't attack them that round. Things that give Advantage (once each): Cordelia's diary · Pip's drawing of her smiling · mentioning Edwin Ashby · offering to visit her (and meaning it) · showing her real art of their own.

Success: Cordelia lowers her brush. She takes the fresh paint signature off her portrait, signs it again in her own hand, and the curse breaks. Pip, Mortimer, and every stolen thing return to the real castle. Cordelia becomes a friendly portrait, and in later years she can watch through any painting in Hollowmere and help the students. Award +20 points to every student involved.

Three failures: she screams, "You're just like them!" and the fight resumes. Players can try again once she's below half her remaining HP.

If She's Defeated

If the Shade drops to 0 HP, the paint melts and the portrait goes blank and grey. Pip and the stolen things return. The students win, but Edwin the ghost is heartbroken, and the castle feels a little sadder. (In later years, a blank frame hangs in the East Wing.)

The Sigil

Read aloudAs the paint settles, you notice something on the back of the easel. Someone has burned a small mark into the wood: a closed eye inside a ring. It's still warm.

Don't explain it. If students ask the Headmistress, she goes very quiet, says "Leave that with me," and changes the subject. This is the first thread of the campaign's big villain.

End of Year

Back in Lantern Hall, the Headmistress gives the students who solved the mystery +50 points for their houses (+30 if Cordelia was defeated rather than saved).

Tally the Lantern Cup. The winning house's banners cover the hall, and its players start Year Two with Heroic Inspiration.

Rewards to hand out: Hodge Barrow gives the students who saved Mortimer a copy of the Spare East Wing Key (and stops reporting them for curfew "as long as I don't see it"). Cordelia (if saved) gives one student Cordelia's Brush. Everyone keeps the item they rolled on the Lost & Found table.

The sky-ferry carries everyone home for summer. On the last page of Pip's new sketchbook, she's drawn all of them together.

Traits

Living Portrait. Cordelia's self-portrait stands on an easel in her studio (AC 10, HP 15; Immunity to Poison and Psychic damage; water or turpentine deals 2d6 Force damage to it). While the portrait is intact, the Shade regains 5 Hit Points at the start of each of its turns if it has at least 1 Hit Point. If the portrait is destroyed, this trait stops working, and the Shade has Disadvantage on attack rolls until the end of its next turn.

Tears of Paint. The first time the Shade is Bloodied, the paint in the room runs. Each creature on the ground must succeed on a DC 12 Dexterity saving throw or have the Prone condition. The Shade then hesitates and doesn't use Paint Them In until the end of its next turn.

Actions

Multiattack. The Shade makes two Brush Stroke attacks.

Brush Stroke. Melee or Ranged Attack Roll: +6, reach 5 ft. or range 60 ft. Hit: 13 (2d8 + 4) Force damage.

Paint Them In (Recharge 5–6). Charisma Saving Throw: DC 14, one creature the Shade can see within 30 feet. Failure: 14 (4d6) Psychic damage, and the target has the Restrained condition as wet paint covers it. Success: Half damage only. A Restrained target, or a creature within 5 feet of it, can take an action to scrub off the paint, ending the condition.

Bonus Actions

Brushstep. The Shade teleports up to 30 feet to an unoccupied space it can see.

The lonely, cursed spirit of the artist Cordelia Vane, woken by a stranger's fresh signature. She can be saved.

VI. Treasure & Items

Common potion

Mendwell Draught

A Hollowmere Potion of Healing. When you drink it, you regain 2d4 + 2 Hit Points. Drinking or giving it is a Bonus Action.

Uncommon wondrous item

Cordelia's Brush

A paintbrush whose tip is always wet. As a Magic action, you can paint a simple nonmagical object no bigger than a 1-foot cube (a key, a rope, a cup, a lantern). The object becomes real for 1 hour, then fades into paint. Once used, the brush can't be used this way again until the next dawn. Painted portraits in Hollowmere treat the holder as a friend.

Common wondrous item

Spare East Wing Key

Hodge Barrow's spare key. It opens the East Wing doors and smells faintly of toad.
